Play Chess Online

This chess game runs in your browser. You do not need to download anything or make an account. Pick a mode, and the board is ready at once. You play White and move first when you play the computer. In two-player mode, two friends share one phone, tablet or computer and take turns.

The game follows the standard international rules, including castling, en passant, pawn promotion, check, checkmate and the usual draws. Legal moves are shown as dots, so you can learn the rules while you play. Illegal moves are not possible.

How to Play Chess (Rules)

  1. 1

    The goal

    Trap the other side's king so it cannot escape. This is called checkmate. White moves first, then players take turns.

  2. 2

    How the pieces move

    Rooks move in straight lines. Bishops move on diagonals. Queens do both. Knights jump in an L shape. Kings move one square. Pawns move forward one square, or two on their first move, and capture one square diagonally.

  3. 3

    Check

    If a king is attacked, the player must get out of check right away. You cannot make a move that leaves your own king attacked.

  4. 4

    Special moves

    Castling moves the king two squares toward a rook, and the rook jumps over. It is only allowed if neither piece has moved, the squares between are empty, and the king does not pass through check. En passant lets a pawn capture a pawn that just moved two squares. A pawn that reaches the last row becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight of your choice.

  5. 5

    Draws

    The game is a draw by stalemate, when the player to move has no legal move but is not in check. It is also a draw when neither side has enough pieces to checkmate, when the same position appears three times, or after 50 moves by each side without a capture or pawn move.

Game Modes and Difficulty

Choose Play vs Computer and then a level. Easy plays legal moves but often misses good ones, so a beginner can win. Medium looks a few moves ahead and avoids simple blunders. Hard searches deeper and plays its best within about a second of thinking time. The computer follows the same rules as you and never cheats. Choose 2 Players to play a friend on the same screen. This is pass and play on one device. It is not online multiplayer.

Chess Tips and Strategy

Control the center with your pawns and knights early. Develop your knights and bishops before you move the queen. Castle early to keep your king safe. Before every move, check what your opponent's last move attacks.

Common mistakes are moving the same piece many times in the opening, leaving pieces undefended and ignoring simple threats. Chess has been played for over 1,400 years and grew from an Indian game called chaturanga. Practice against Easy, then move up a level when you win.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is chess?

Chess is a two-player strategy game on an 8 by 8 board. Each side has 16 pieces, and the goal is to checkmate the opposing king.

How do I play chess here?

Choose a mode, click one of your pieces to see where it can go, then click a highlighted square. In two-player mode, players take turns on the same device.

Does this game use the real chess rules?

Yes. It includes castling, en passant, promotion, check, checkmate, stalemate, insufficient material, threefold repetition and the 50-move rule.

Is this chess game free?

Yes. It is free to play, with no download and no sign-up.

Can I play chess with a friend?

Yes, on one device. Choose 2 Players and take turns. There is no online multiplayer.

What are the difficulty levels?

Easy makes frequent mistakes. Medium thinks a few moves ahead. Hard searches deeper and plays its best. The computer never cheats at any level.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. The board fits phone screens and works with touch, mouse and keyboard.

What is a good tip for beginners?

Control the center, develop your minor pieces, castle early and always check what your opponent is threatening before you move.

Can I pick a different piece when my pawn reaches the end?

Yes. When your pawn reaches the last row, you choose a queen, rook, bishop or knight. The computer chooses a queen.
