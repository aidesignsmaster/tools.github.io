Tips for Smaller Files Without Visible Loss

Photos compress best as JPG or WebP. Screenshots with flat colors and text may look better as PNG, but they only get smaller if you reduce the width. If a website needs an image shown at 800 px wide, there is no reason to upload a 4000 px original, so set a maximum width first and compress after.

The smart mix strategy balances quality and dimensions. Choose Keep dimensions if the pixel size must stay the same, or Keep quality high if you would rather shrink the picture than soften it.

When Compression Helps

Smaller images load faster on your website, fit inside email attachment limits and pass the file size limits of job portals, exam forms and government sites. They also save phone and cloud storage. For websites, a lighter page usually feels faster to visitors, which is good for them and for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an image compressor?

It is a tool that makes an image file smaller by re-encoding it, usually with a little less detail that is hard to notice. This one runs inside your browser, so nothing needs to be installed.

How do I compress an image to a specific size like 50 KB?

Upload your image, keep the mode on Exact target size, pick a size such as 50 KB and press Compress. The tool searches for the highest quality that still stays under your limit.

Can I compress several images at once?

Yes. You can add up to 50 images, compress them in one go and download them together as a ZIP file.

Will compression make my image blurry?

Moderate compression is rarely visible. If you ask for a very small file the tool has to lower quality or dimensions, and you can choose which one it sacrifices first.

Which format should I choose?

JPG suits photos and works everywhere. WebP is usually smaller at the same quality. PNG is lossless, so the only way to shrink it is to reduce its dimensions.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Compression happens on your device using your browser, so your files never leave it.
