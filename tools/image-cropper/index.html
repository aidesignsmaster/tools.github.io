How to Crop an Image

  1. Add imagesSelect, drag in or paste up to 50 images.
  2. Choose a shapePick a ratio or Free Crop. You can also type exact width and height in pixels.
  3. Adjust the cropDrag inside the box to move it and drag a corner to resize. Arrow keys nudge it by one pixel, and Shift makes the step ten.
  4. SaveChoose JPG, PNG or WebP, then crop the current image or apply the crop to all images and download the results.

Common Aspect Ratios

1:1 suits profile pictures and many social posts. 4:5 is a tall portrait format used for feed posts, 9:16 fits stories and short videos, 16:9 matches widescreen video and banners, and 3:2 is the classic photo shape. Pick the ratio first, then place the box, so the framing stays right.

When Cropping Helps

Cropping removes distractions, centers the subject and gives the picture the exact shape a platform expects, such as a square profile photo, a story, a thumbnail or a banner. If you also need exact pixel dimensions for the whole picture rather than a cut-out, the Image Resizer is the better fit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this image cropper free?

Yes. It is free, needs no account and has no watermark.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Cropping happens in your browser, so your files stay on your device.

How do I crop to a specific aspect ratio?

Choose a ratio such as 1:1, 4:5 or 16:9, or enter your own width and height ratio. The crop box keeps that shape while you move and resize it.

Can I crop many images the same way?

Yes. Set the crop on one image and press Apply to All Images. The same relative area is used on every image in the list, and a fixed ratio is respected.

Can I rotate or flip before cropping?

Yes. Use the rotate and flip buttons, then position the crop on the corrected image.

Will cropping reduce image quality?

The pixels inside the crop are copied as they are. Quality only changes if you save as JPG or WebP below 100%, so use PNG or a high quality value to stay lossless or nearly so.
