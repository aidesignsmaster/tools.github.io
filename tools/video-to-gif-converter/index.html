Make a Clean Animated GIF From Any Video

Video to GIF Converter turns part of a video into an animated GIF without uploading anything. Pick the exact start and end, choose the width and frame rate, change the speed, play it forward, in reverse or as a boomerang, crop it to a square, add a caption and apply a simple colour effect. The result has no watermark and loops forever or plays once, as you choose.

GIF files can grow large, so the tool works to keep them small. It chooses the best 255 colours for your clip instead of using a fixed palette, and it stores only the parts of the picture that change from one frame to the next.

How to Use It

  1. Select or drop a video. Drag the markers or type times to choose the moment you want, or use Start = Now and End = Now while it plays.
  2. Set the width, frame rate, speed and direction. Add a caption or a crop if you like.
  3. Press Convert to GIF and watch the progress bar.
  4. Check the preview and download the GIF.

Practical Tips

Keep it short. Two to five seconds is ideal. Pick the width to match the use. 320 or 480 pixels suits chats and email, while 640 or more suits websites. Use 10 to 15 frames per second for smooth motion with a modest size, and only go higher for fast action. Leave Optimize file size on unless you need every pixel exact. For flat graphics such as screen recordings, turn dithering off for the cleanest, smallest result.

When It Helps

Use it to make a reaction GIF from a clip, a short demo of an app or product, a looping highlight for social media, or an animated step in a tutorial. It also suits chat apps and emails where a short silent loop works better than a video.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my video uploaded to a server?

No. The video is read and converted inside your browser, so it never leaves your device. The GIF has no watermark and no sign-up is needed.

Which video formats work?

MP4, WebM and MOV work as long as your browser can play them. MP4 with H.264 video is the safest choice. If a file will not open, try converting it to MP4 first.

How do I make the GIF file smaller?

Shorten the clip, choose a smaller width such as 320 or 480 pixels, lower the frame rate to 8 or 10, and keep Optimize file size ticked. Turning dithering off also helps, especially on flat graphics.

How long can the GIF be?

The limit depends on the size you choose. At 480 pixels wide you can make a few hundred frames, for example about 30 seconds at 10 frames per second. The page tells you the frame count and the limit before you convert.

Can I reverse it, loop it or add text?

Yes. Choose Reverse or Boomerang under Direction, pick Loop forever or Play once, and type a caption to place meme-style text at the top or bottom. You can also crop to a square or other shape and apply a grayscale or sepia look.

Why do the colours look a little different from the video?

A GIF can hold only 256 colours per image. The tool picks the best 255 colours for your particular clip and can add light dithering to smooth gradients, but photos and videos will always look slightly less rich as a GIF.
