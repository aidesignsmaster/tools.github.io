Copy and Paste Colorful Emoji and Emoji Combos

Everyone knows the emoji on their keyboard, but the best-looking messages use emoji combos: two or three emoji placed side by side, like a tiny picture. This page puts 400 hand-picked emoji and combos in one place and sorts them by mood and topic, so you can find a good one in seconds. Tap the Copy button next to a design and paste it into any chat, comment, caption or bio.

If you like to mix several designs, use the + button. Each design you add lands in the My Mix box at the top, where you can edit it and copy everything in one tap.

How to Copy and Paste an Emoji

  1. Pick a category chip such as Love or Food, or type a word like “cat” or “good morning” in the search box.
  2. Tap Copy under the emoji you like. The button turns green when it is copied.
  3. To collect several, tap the + button on each one. They appear in the My Mix box at the top, where you can edit them. Then tap Copy All.
  4. Open your chat, comment box or bio, press and hold, and choose Paste.

What Are Emoji Combos?

An emoji combo is a short row of emoji that works together, for example a sun, a coffee cup and a flower for a good morning message. Combos make captions, bios and chats feel more personal than a single emoji, and they are easy to copy because each one is just text that your phone draws as a small picture.

Where You Can Use Them

You can paste these emoji into chats, social media captions and comments, profile bios, game names, email subject lines, notes and documents. Most apps accept them because they are normal text characters. A few apps do not allow emoji in usernames, and then the paste will simply not work there.

Tips for Better Results

Keep it short. One or two combos say more than a whole row. Expect small differences. Emoji pictures look a little different on iPhone, Android and Windows, because each system draws its own artwork. Pick another design if you see a box. A very old phone may not have a newer emoji, so we only use emoji that almost every phone supports. Do not overdo it in names and bios. Screen readers read every emoji aloud, which can be tiring for people who use them.
