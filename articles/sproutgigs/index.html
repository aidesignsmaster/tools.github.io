SproutGigs Review: Micro Jobs, Levels and Payouts

By Working Tools Box • Updated November 2026

Earnings are never guaranteed and vary by country and effort. Rules, minimum payouts and available tasks can change, so check the official site before you start. Some links may be referral links, which can give us a small bonus at no cost to you.

Quick facts

  • Type: micro jobs and freelance gigs marketplace, plus paid surveys
  • Cost to join: free
  • Task prices seen: about $0.03 to $2.55 on starter jobs
  • Our own average: about $0.076 per task with an 89% success rate
  • Minimum withdrawal: we believe around $5, confirm on your Wallet page

SproutGigs is a marketplace where people pay for small online jobs and freelancers get paid to do them. As a worker you can complete quick tasks such as installing an app, watching a short video or creating an account, or you can sell your own skills as a gig. This guide covers what you will see inside, what tasks pay, how the level and success rate system works, and what to watch out for. It is based on our own account, so we use real numbers from it.

What is SproutGigs?

The site has two main parts. Micro Jobs are small tasks posted by buyers, and you are paid a few cents or dollars when the buyer approves your work. Gigs work like a freelance marketplace, where sellers list services such as article writing or website setup at a fixed price. There is also a Paid Surveys section. One account can switch between buyer and freelancer, and the menu has a Share & Earn page if you want to invite others.

What jobs can you do?

When we checked, the Micro Jobs list showed 375 jobs. You can filter by job level, category, payment and location, and sort by most recent. The categories include sign up tasks, SEO and engagement, data entry and collection, Telegram, Discord, mobile apps, writing an article, leads, AI, and surveys and offers. Some jobs are marked Featured, Express or Power Job.

These are example prices we saw on the list:

  • Install an app and log in: about $0.175.
  • Create a Gmail or other account: about $0.10 to $0.135.
  • Reddit upvote task: about $0.08.
  • SEO and engage task: about $0.03.
  • Watch a short video: one power job paid $2.55, and some app install tasks paid $1.00.

Each job also shows how many spots are left, for example 570 of 4,290 remaining, and a review time such as 1 day or 7 days. Good jobs run out fast, so check the list often.

How a job works

  1. Open a job and read the instructions fully.
  2. Do exactly what the buyer asks and submit the proof they ask for.
  3. The buyer rates your work as Satisfied or Not Satisfied.
  4. Satisfied work is paid into your wallet. Money waiting for approval shows as Pending.

The page also shows a 30 second timer, which is the minimum time between submitting tasks.

Levels and success rate: the part most beginners miss

SproutGigs ranks freelancers by level. Your level decides which jobs you can open, and higher levels get access to higher paying jobs. You gain points for approved tasks, and the rules on the Freelancer Stats page say you earn 40 points for each $1 you earn. Bad work costs you: a task rated Not Satisfied removes 50 points times your level, and a task rated spam or duplicate removes 200 times your level. A week of inactivity also removes points.

Your success rate matters as well. The page shows an all-time rate and a temporary rate, and it says that workers with a long record of good work can keep submitting even if their temporary rate drops below 75%. In practice this means careless submissions can slow you down, so quality beats speed.

What did we earn?

On our own account we completed 62 tasks, with 49 rated Satisfied and 4 rated Not Satisfied, giving an all-time success rate of 89%. We earned about $3.73 in total, which is around $0.076 per task. That is typical for starter level jobs. It is a small side income, and it grows only if you keep your quality high and move up in level.

Honest note: we have not requested a withdrawal yet, so we cannot describe payout speed from our own experience. Check the Wallet page before you start.

Withdrawals

The Wallet page has tabs for Withdrawals, Transactions and My Crypto Addresses, plus a Request Withdrawal button. On our account that button is grey until the balance is high enough. We believe the minimum is around $5, but confirm the exact amount, the available payout methods and any fees on your own Wallet page, because these can change. You can also add a recovery email and complete identity verification in the account settings, which is a good idea before you withdraw.

Tips to earn more

  • Start with simple starter jobs that clearly explain what proof is needed.
  • Take a screenshot or note of each step before you submit.
  • Do not submit a job twice. Duplicates are punished heavily.
  • Check back often, because the best jobs have limited spots.
  • Stay active every week so you do not lose level points.

Things to be careful about

  • Face verification and app installs: some jobs ask you to verify your face or log in to another app. Only share personal data with apps you trust.
  • Fake accounts: jobs that ask you to create many accounts may break the rules of the other website and put your own accounts at risk.
  • Gigs: some gigs promise to raise website authority scores or traffic. Search engines dislike artificial boosting, so avoid these for your own site.
  • Payments: never pay anyone to unlock jobs or raise your level.

Who is SproutGigs good for?

SproutGigs suits people who like quick online tasks and can follow instructions carefully. Freelancers with a skill, such as writing or design, can also sell a gig. It is not a good choice if you want fast money or do not want to deal with sign ups on other sites.

Our verdict

SproutGigs offers a large list of real jobs and a clear level system, but the starter jobs pay very little. Treat it as a way to build a small side income, protect your success rate and move up in level for better jobs.

Frequently asked questions

Why was my task rejected?

Buyers rate work as Satisfied or Not Satisfied. Missing steps, wrong proof or duplicate submissions are common reasons.

Yes. The Gigs section lets freelancers list services such as writing, design or website setup at a fixed price.
