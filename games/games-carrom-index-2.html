Carrom is a flick-and-pocket board game loved in homes, clubs and tea shops across South Asia. Here you can play carrom online in your browser with no download and no sign-up. The board is drawn in 3D, the coins slide and crash with fast physics, and the board stays still while each player slides the striker along their own line. This version gives you more choice than most. There are 20 different strikers, and each one has its own power, size and weight. How to Play Carrom (Rules)

  1. 1

    Set up the board

    The board has four corner pockets, four baselines and a centre circle. There are 9 coins for each side and one red queen in the middle, in the standard flower pattern. The first side shoots from the bottom baseline. In 4 player games, partners sit opposite each other and the turn goes Player 1, 2, 3, 4.

  2. 2

    Place and shoot the striker

    On your turn, put the striker anywhere on your baseline between the two end circles. Use the slider or drag along the baseline. Then touch the striker, pull back and release. The farther you pull, the more power you use. The striker cannot start on top of a coin.

  3. 3

    Pocket your own coins

    You only score with your own colour. If you pocket one of your coins, you shoot again. If you pocket one of your opponent's coins, it stays pocketed for them and your turn ends. If nothing is pocketed, the turn passes.

  4. 4

    Avoid fouls

    If the striker falls into a pocket, it is a foul and your turn ends. Any coin pocketed on that shot also goes back to the centre. One of your pocketed coins goes back to the centre. If you have not pocketed any coin yet, you owe one: the next coin you pocket goes back to the centre.

  5. 5

    Win the queen

    You may pocket the queen only after you have pocketed one of your own coins, or if you pocket one of your coins in the same shot. You must cover the queen by pocketing one of your coins on that shot or on your very next shot. If you do not, the queen goes back to the centre. If you pocket your last coin before the queen, that coin goes back to the centre.

  6. 6

    Win the game

    The first side to pocket all 9 of its coins, with the queen covered, wins. If 30 shots in a row pocket nothing, the side with more coins pocketed wins, and equal sides draw.

Game Modes and Difficulty

Choose Play vs Computer to face one opponent. On Easy, the computer tries only a few ideas and its aim is loose, so it misses good chances and a beginner can beat it. On Medium, it tests many more shots in its head, picks a sensible one and aims more carefully, with only small slips. On Hard, it tests a large number of shots, plays the best one it finds and aims almost perfectly. The computer never cheats. It uses the same physics, the same rules and the same 20 strikers that you can use. It cannot see anything you cannot see.

Choose 2 Players to play with a friend on the same device, or 4 Players for two teams of two. The board turns to face each player, and the turn pill shows whose go it is. Before the game starts, every player picks a striker and each side picks its coin colour on the set-up screen. Once the game begins these choices are locked, so nobody can swap strikers or colours in the middle of a game.

Carrom Tips and Strategy

Start by breaking the cluster in the middle with a strong, straight shot from the centre of your baseline. A heavy, high power striker spreads the coins and gives you open shots. After that, switch to a lighter striker for careful work near the pockets. Aim for coins that sit close to a pocket, and use the white guide dots to see which coin you will touch first. The yellow dashed line shows where that coin will start moving, so line it up with a pocket.

The common mistakes are using too much power near a pocket and pocketing the striker by accident. A fast striker keeps moving after it hits a coin, so it can follow the coin into the hole. Try a smaller pull when the pocket is close. Do not rush the queen either. Pocket one of your own coins first, and keep a second coin ready as cover. Carrom began on the Indian subcontinent and is played at home, in clubs and in international tournaments, where the same ideas of angle, power and patience decide most games.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is carrom?

Carrom is a board game where players flick a striker to knock coins into the four corner pockets. Each side has 9 coins of its own colour, and there is one red queen. The first side to pocket all its coins and cover the queen wins.

How do I play carrom here?

Choose a mode, place the striker on your baseline with the slider or by dragging, then touch the striker, pull back and let go. The farther you pull, the stronger the shot. A white dotted guide shows where the striker will go.

What are the rules of carrom in this game?

You pocket only your own colour. Pocketing your coin gives another shot. An opponent's coin stays pocketed and ends your turn. If the striker falls in, it is a foul and a coin returns to the centre. The queen must be covered on the same shot or the next one.

Is this carrom game free?

Yes. It is free to play, with no download, no sign-up and no payment.

Can I play carrom with friends on one device?

Yes. Choose 2 Players or 4 Players and pass the device around. In 4 Player mode, partners sit opposite each other and play as a team. This is not online multiplayer. Everyone plays on the same device.

What do Easy, Medium and Hard mean?

They set how well the computer plays. Easy tries few shots and misses often. Medium tests many shots and aims carefully. Hard tests the most shots and aims almost perfectly. The computer follows the same rules and physics as you.

Does carrom work on mobile?

Yes. The board fits phone screens, and you play with touch. Drag the striker back to aim and release to shoot. It also works with a mouse, and keyboard keys can move and shoot the striker.

What is the best striker to use?

There is no single best striker. High power strikers are heavier and bigger, so they break the cluster well but are harder to control near the pockets. Lighter strikers are smaller and easier to place for careful shots. Try several and use the one you aim best with.

Why did my queen go back to the centre?

The queen goes back if you pocket it before pocketing any of your own coins, if you fail to cover it with one of your coins on the same or next shot, or if the striker falls in on that shot.
