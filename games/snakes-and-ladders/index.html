How to Play Snakes & Ladders (Rules)

  1. 1

    Start and take turns

    Every pawn begins off the board, on the START ledge. Players take turns in order. In Play vs Computer you are Player 1 and the computer is Player 2. In the friends modes, 2 or 4 players share one device.

  2. 2

    Roll and move

    On your turn, tap Roll Dice. The 3D dice shows a number from 1 to 6, and your pawn moves forward that many squares. The squares are numbered 1 to 100, starting at the bottom left. Each row runs in the opposite direction to the row below it, in a zig-zag.

  3. 3

    Ladders go up

    If your pawn ends its move on the bottom of a ladder, it climbs to the top. The game has 10 boards, and on the Classic board the ladders are 1 to 38, 4 to 14, 9 to 31, 21 to 42, 28 to 84, 36 to 44, 51 to 67, 71 to 91 and 80 to 100.

  4. 4

    Snakes go down

    If your pawn ends its move on a snake's head, it slides down to the tail. On the Classic board the snakes are 16 to 6, 47 to 26, 49 to 11, 56 to 53, 62 to 19, 64 to 60, 87 to 24, 93 to 73, 95 to 75 and 98 to 78.

  5. 5

    Roll a 6, roll again

    Whenever you roll a 6, you get another roll after your move is finished. There is no limit on how many times in a row this can happen.

  6. 6

    Win on square 100

    You must land exactly on square 100. If your roll is too big, your pawn stays where it is and the turn passes (a 6 still gives you another roll). The ladder from 80 also takes you to 100 and wins. The first pawn to reach 100 wins the game.

Game Modes and Difficulty

Play vs Computer: you play against one automatic opponent. The computer rolls its own dice, with the same fair dice and the same rules as you. It does not cheat. Because Snakes & Ladders has no decisions to make, there are no Easy, Medium or Hard levels here, and we do not pretend there are. The only thing that matters is luck.

2 Players and 4 Players: these modes are for friends on the same device. Everyone takes turns on one screen. Pass the phone or laptop to the next player when the turn changes. The turn badge above the board shows whose go it is, and each pawn has its own colour and number so players can tell them apart. This is not online multiplayer, and players do not connect from different devices.

The dice is fair. Each roll uses the secure random generator of your browser, in the same way for humans and for the computer, with a method that gives all six numbers exactly the same chance.

Snakes & Ladders Tips and Strategy

Snakes & Ladders is a game of luck, so no tactic can change what the dice shows. Still, a few facts make the game more fun. The average roll is 3.5, so a pawn moves a little under four squares per turn. Because of the snakes and the ladders, the number of rolls needed changes a lot from game to game. In our tests, one pawn needed about 39 rolls on average. A lucky climb can finish much faster, and a few very unlucky games took more than 100 rolls.

Learn the big ones. The ladder from 28 to 84 is the largest jump on the Classic board, and the ladder from 80 to 100 wins the game on the spot. Near the end, the snake from 98 to 78 and the snake from 87 to 24 are the cruelest. Knowing them helps you cheer or groan at the right time.

Common mistakes: many new players think a big roll finishes the game. You need to land exactly on 100. Another mistake is to think that a ladder works when you pass it. It only works when your move ends on the bottom square of the ladder.

A little history: Snakes and ladders is often traced to ancient India, where it was played as Moksha Patam. Ladders stood for good deeds and snakes stood for bad ones, so the game taught a lesson about life. The game reached England in the 1800s, and a version called Chutes and Ladders became popular in the United States in the 1940s.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Snakes & Ladders?

Snakes & Ladders is a classic dice board game. Players race from the start to square 100. Ladders lift you up the board, and snakes drop you back down. The first player to reach 100 wins.

How do I play Snakes & Ladders online here?

Open the page, choose Play vs Computer, 2 Players or 4 Players, and the game starts at once. On your turn, tap Roll Dice. Your pawn moves by itself. Ladders and snakes are followed for you.

What are the rules of Snakes & Ladders?

Roll one die and move that many squares. A ladder takes you up, and a snake takes you down. A roll of 6 gives you another roll. You must land exactly on square 100 to win. If your roll is too high, you stay where you are.

Is this Snakes & Ladders game free?

Yes. It is free to play, with no download, no account and no sign-up. It works in your browser.

Can I play with friends on one device?

Yes. Choose 2 Players or 4 Players and pass the device from turn to turn. This is play on the same device. It is not online multiplayer.

Does the computer have difficulty levels?

No. In Snakes & Ladders the dice decides every move, so there is nothing for the computer to get better at. The computer uses the same fair dice and the same rules as you, so no levels are needed.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. The 3D board scales to small screens, and the Roll Dice button is large enough to tap easily. It works on phones, tablets and computers.

Is there a strategy tip for Snakes & Ladders?

There are no choices to make, so there is no winning strategy. The best tip is to enjoy the ride and learn the ladders and snakes, so you know what each square can do.

Do I need an exact roll to reach square 100?

Yes. You must land exactly on 100. If your roll would take you past 100, your pawn stays where it is. A 6 still gives another roll, and the ladder from square 80 takes you straight to 100.
